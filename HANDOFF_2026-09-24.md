## RETRACTIONS. READ THESE FIRST.

### 1. THE SIGMA FLOOR DOES NOT EXIST

`PARAMS["receptions"]["floor"] = 1.7` is a **projection** floor. `p_over`
reads it here:

```python
if proj < PARAMS[market]["floor"]:
    return None
```

A projection below 1.7 returns `None` and renders unpriced. It is not a floor
on sigma. The only sigma floor in `mc_pricing` is `SIGMA_MIN = 0.05`, which
never binds at any market's scale.

The shipped receptions sigma is `1.143 + 0.2992 * mean`, **unfloored**.

`sigma_refit.py` line 92 hardcodes the misreading:

```
SIGMA_A, SIGMA_B, SIGMA_FLOOR = 1.143, 0.2992, 1.7
```

and then lines 46 to 51 reason forward from it, describing the 1.7 as a guard
against negative binomial dispersion below 1. That is why the analysis went
looking for a zero-atom pathology and found one: it constructed the form that
would cause it.

Worse, `sigma_refit` DID evaluate the real shipped form. It sits in the
candidate list as `Form("shipped-nofloor", ...)` at line 180, described as
"isolates the floor's contribution", which framed the actual production
behaviour as a counterfactual and put a fiction in the baseline slot.

**Retract:** headline 2 of the September 23 handoff in full; the 22.47 pp
figure and every floored row of that table; the mean-0.8 worked example; both
cross-market claims; the plan's "why the pricing layer moved up" rationale;
plan item 3.2; the three cautions under plan 4.2; order-of-operations items 2
and 3 in the new-directions document.

### 2. THE CROSS-MARKET FLOOR CLAIM WAS IMPOSSIBLE, NOT JUST WRONG

Rushing and qb_passing are `family="gamma"`. A gamma has no dispersion
parameter and no zero atom, so "82.2 percent of rows with dispersion below 1"
cannot have been computed from `mc_pricing` for rushing.

qb_passing's reported symptom, that a floor of 159.0 collapses all 1,731 rows
into the single band 0.35 to 0.45, is a fingerprint of the misreading:
`max(0.3531 * proj, 159)` equals 159 for every quarterback under 450 yards.

**And the symptom reproduces from something real.** With beta clipped to zero,
qb_passing prices every prop at the line and lands in one band. That is
visible in today's grid: `B blend / P params` gives 1,662 rows in 0.45 to
0.50 and nothing elsewhere. So the observation was genuine and the
attribution was wrong.

**Plan item 4.6, the 281-row bottom band, is resolved by this.** Those rows are

## THE EVALUATION HARNESS HAS NEVER MEASURED THE APP'S MODEL

Discovered because the Phase 3.4 edit produced harness output **byte-identical
to the baseline**, which is arithmetically impossible for a change that adds
3,960 training rows.

`eval_harness.py` does not call `load_model`. It fits its own regression:

```
452: model = LinearRegression().fit(train[list(feats)], train[actual_col])
```

and its own docstring lists the fix as future work:

```
92: prerequisite for item 3, switching to each module's own load_model.
```

The modules are used only for `build_dataset` and the feature list.

**Three consequences.**

**Beta 0.275 describes the harness's fit, not the app's model.** Every
instruction across three handoffs phrased as "run the harness before and after
this model change" was unexecutable.

**The harness applies no volume gate at all.** Its training set is
`df[df["season"] < season]` after a feature `dropna`. No `targets_roll`
filter. So it already trains looser than Phase 3.4 proposed, ungated, while
`load_model` trains at 3 and serves at 1.5.

**There is a hidden 2022 training season.** `build_dataset` returns 2022
through 2026 (5,437 / 5,390 / 5,444 / 5,629 / 640). 2022 is never scored
because there is no line data for it, but it is what trains 2023. So the 2023
beta of 0.243 rests on a single training season.

### The parity measurement

Fitting the harness's way (ungated, `season <= 2024`) against `load_model`'s
way (gated at 3, same window), then comparing the 296 served projections for
2025 week 10:

| | n_train | mean abs diff | max abs diff |
|---|---|---|---|
| gate 3 vs gate 1.5 | 7,280 vs 11,240 | 0.058 | 0.137 |
| gate 3 vs ungated (harness) | 7,280 vs 15,477 | 0.106 | 0.316 |

Coefficients differ substantially, including a 1.11-reception intercept gap,
yet the functions agree to about a tenth of a reception where the players
actually live. 14 of 296 players exceed 0.25, none exceeds 0.5.

### ARCHITECTURE DECISION: LEAVE THE HARNESS ALONE

**Beta 0.275 is a fair PROXY for the app's model, not a description of it.**
Write that down and stop treating it as either.

Making the harness call `load_model` was considered and rejected.
`load_model` is a single fit pinned to `season <= 2024`. The harness refits
walk-forward per season so that nothing is scored on rows it was fitted on. If
it called `load_model` it would score 2023 and 2024 with a model trained on
them, destroying the leave-season-out discipline that makes every number in
the project trustworthy. That is a real cost for a proxy error of 0.1
receptions.

**The harness measures forecast skill of a feature set, fitted honestly. It
does not measure the deployed artifact. Those are different questions and
conflating them is what produced three handoffs of dead instructions.**

The missing piece is a parity check, run manually twice today. Worth making a
script before the next model change.

### A measurement trap worth keeping

The first pass at this used `project_week`'s output, which is rounded:

to the 0.1 grid, producing "105 of 296 players over 0.10" which was entirely a
rounding artifact. On unrounded predictions the count over 0.10 is a handful.

**Never measure a small effect through a rounded output.**

## PHASE 4.4 IS SHIPPED. THE MEAN WAS THE DEFECT.

The pricing layer now prices `line + alpha + beta * (projection - line)`
instead of the raw projection.

| market | worst band, raw proj | worst band, blend |
|---|---|---|
| receptions | +21.1 | **+2.4** |
| receiving | -18.4 | **-3.8** |
| rushing | +31.7 | **-1.5** |
| qb_passing | -20.6 | **-5.3** |

Sigma unchanged in all four. **The over-dispersion tilt was mean error in
every market**, which is exactly what the September 23 `dP/dsigma` finding
predicted: sigma shifts every band the same direction and can never move the
two ends in opposite directions.

### The shipped constants

```
market       alpha      beta     basis
receptions   +0.1491    0.2261   direct, SE 0.042; beta t +6.5
receiving    +3.4623    0.0      direct, SE 0.634; beta clipped (t +1.5)
rushing      +4.0067    0.0      direct, SE 0.976; beta clipped (t -0.9)
qb_passing    0.0       0.0      alpha unresolvable, SE 2.55
qb_rushing    0.0       0.0      no data, MARKET_MAP label bug
```

**Beta is clipped to zero wherever the game-clustered CI includes zero.** Only
receptions survives. Shipping rushing's -0.043 would price AWAY from the
projection. Shipping receiving's 0.066 or qb_passing's 0.085 would display
phantom edges in markets with no signal. The harness's own out-of-sample
procedure already clips rushing to 0.000.

**Alpha is the DIRECT measurement at zero deviation, not the regression
intercept.** Once beta is clipped, the intercept of a free-slope fit is the
wrong parameter: it carries a correction for a slope the model no longer has.
Rushing shows the gap plainly, intercept +3.46 against direct +4.01.

**Dropping alpha was tested and is much worse.** Pricing the mean at the line
understates P(over) systematically, because the outcome mean genuinely sits
above a line placed near the median. Receiving's worst band goes -3.8 to -8.8
and **7,858 of 7,861 props land in the top tier.** Alpha is what prevents
that.

**qb_passing alpha is 0 because it is unresolvable, not absent.** Direct alpha
+0.55, SE 2.55, per-season +1.37 / +4.68 / -3.94. Sigma there is about 73
yards, so a few yards of skew is one fortieth of a standard deviation and
1,670 rows cannot pin it. Pricing at the line is honest; shipping +2.37 as
though it were known is not.

### PHASE 4.2 IS CLOSED AS NOT AN IMPROVEMENT

Three sigma forms tested against the blend. The shipped `PARAMS` forms won in
every market:

| market | PARAMS | flat residSD | sqrt(1.22 m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| receptions | **+2.4** | +5.5 | +7.4 |
| receiving | **-3.8** | -20.2 | |
| rushing | **-1.5** | +3.3 | |
| qb_passing | -5.3 | **-5.1** | |

`eval_harness`'s legend says its `residSD` is the correct sigma for pricing

is not the same thing as residual spread conditional on the mean.

**Do not touch sigma without re-running `blend_sigma_grid.py` first.**

## TWO NEW BUGS IN READERS OF `lines`

### `get_lines` never filtered on book. This fed the PRICED line.

`get_lines` builds `{player: {line, over_odds, ...}}` by overwriting a dict as
rows come back oldest-first, filtering only on sport, season, week and market.
With an eight-book capture, **the prefilled line was whichever book happened
to write last at the latest `captured_at`**, which is arbitrary. It also
included hand-saved rows.

This function populates the editable Line column, so an arbitrary book's line
is what got priced. Fixed: FanDuel only, saved rows excluded, and it now
returns empty with a warning rather than silently substituting another book.

### The `lines` table contains 5,071 hand-saved board rows

| | |
|---|---|
| total rows | 43,465, 8 books, **2026 only** |
| hand-saved | **5,071** (`book='fanduel'` AND `projection IS NOT NULL`) |
| date span of saved rows | 2026-09-02 to 2026-09-20 |

The Save-to-Log path writes into the same table as the capture. There is **no
column marking a row as saved**; the discriminator is that captured rows have
no `projection` and no `edge` because the capture has no model attached. That
is an accident, not a design. If a future writer populates `projection` on
capture, the fix breaks silently. **Add an explicit source column.**

So `get_line_movement` had TWO independent contaminations, not one: eight
books treated as eight snapshots, AND hand-saved rows interleaved into the
time series. Both fixed.

### The research instrument was never exposed to either

`lines` holds 2026 only. The 354,554 historical rows live in
`lines_cache.parquet` (184,782 rows) and come from The Odds API directly.
`eval_harness` never reads the Supabase table. That is why these bugs never
reached an analysis.

## THE ANCHOR MATTERS, AND IT IS NOW VERIFIED

**Beta is highly sensitive to which line you anchor on.** Across honest
anchors, excluding `line_best`:

| market | FanDuel | consensus | min | mid | max | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| receptions | 0.226 | 0.275 | 0.403 | 0.350 | 0.490 | **0.264** |
| receiving | 0.066 | 0.093 | 0.189 | 0.242 | 0.440 | **0.374** |
| rushing | -0.043 | -0.032 | 0.061 | 0.106 | 0.256 | **0.299** |
| qb_passing | 0.085 | 0.075 | 0.231 | 0.216 | 0.316 | **0.241** |

That spread is 7 to 10 standard errors. `line_min` and `line_max` are order
statistics over eight books, so anchoring on them inflates beta mechanically:
a line further from truth leaves more room for the projection to be right
about the gap. **Same artifact family as `line_best`, BetRivers, and synthetic
consensus.** `line_best` came back visibly inflated in all four markets, which
is a working internal check on the mechanism.

**FanDuel and consensus agree** (0.226 vs 0.275, z -1.07) and are the only two
real anchors.

### Typed lines versus captured lines

The app prices a hand-typed line, so the constants only hold if what gets
typed is FanDuel's line. Measured against the capture:

| market | saved rows | with any capture | line matched | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **receptions** | 1,073 | 384 | 355 | **92.4%** |
| receiving | 1,160 | 417 | 197 | 47.2% |
| rushing | 514 | 207 | 91 | 44.0% |
| qb_passing | 222 | 132 | 59 | 44.7% |

**Receptions matches at 92.4 percent**, so the 0.226 and 0.149 constants are
fitted on the right anchor for the one market that matters.

The yardage markets at 44 to 47 percent are granularity and the `get_lines`
bug: reception lines sit on a coarse half-integer grid where books agree, so
an arbitrary book is usually FanDuel's number anyway; receiving lines run 7.5
to 44.5 in half-yard steps and differ between books routinely. It does not
matter there because beta is zero, so the projection never enters the price
and alpha is defined relative to whatever line was typed.

**This gap is closed prospectively, not retrospectively.** Every calibration
figure in this project describes captured lines. Going forward the typed line
will be FanDuel's because the prefill is fixed.

## WHAT WAS TESTED AND FOUND FLAT

Both parameters cut every way we could think of. All consistent.

### Caliber

| cut | beta p | alpha (direct) p |
|---|---|---|
| target_share_roll quartile | 0.62 | 0.69 |
| position (WR/TE/RB) | | 0.55 |
| exact line value | 0.95 | 0.73 |
| n_books | 0.76 | |

96.2 percent join rate on the target-share cut, so that one is trustworthy.

**`residSD` rises monotonically with caliber on every proxy** (1.64 to 2.51
across target-share quartiles, 1.51 to 2.82 across line values) while beta
does not. Absolute variance grows with caliber even though the slope does not,
which is why sigma must be a function of the mean.

**Beta is if anything slightly LOWER for high-caliber players** (0.294 at Q1
down to 0.210 at Q4). Not significant, but it points opposite to the
quote-frequency spearman +1.00 finding. Those measure different things and the
existing finding is less settled than +1.00 made it sound.

### Game context, receptions, Bonferroni threshold 0.0036

| cut | beta p | alpha p | min detectable beta spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| home/away | 0.64 | 0.68 | 0.10 |
| favourite/underdog | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.16 |
| game total | 0.37 | 0.28 | 0.13 |
| roof | 0.99 | 0.13 | 0.19 |
| weekday | 0.89 | 0.15 | **0.42** |
| divisional | 0.26 | 0.36 | 0.14 |
| rest days | 0.95 | 0.35 | 0.16 |

**Read the minimum detectable spread before calling this settled.** Against a
pooled beta of 0.24, weekday could only detect a difference of 0.42, which is
nearly twice the whole parameter (Saturday has 177 rows, beta SE 0.21). The
honest statement is "no effect larger than 0.10 to 0.42 depending on the cut",
not "no effect".

### A METHODOLOGICAL CATCH WORTH MORE THAN THE RESULTS

**The regression intercept manufactures position effects that vanish at zero
deviation.** Twice:

| market | intercept p | direct p |
|---|---|---|
| receptions | **0.0033** | 0.55 |
| receiving | **0.0391** | 0.81 |

Receptions TE intercept +0.268 against WR +0.070 looks real until measured at
zero deviation, where it is +0.217 against +0.112 with overlapping SEs. The
intercept's SE depends on how far a tier's deviations sit from zero, so a tier
with few near-zero rows gets a wide and biased intercept.

**Plan item 3.7, WR receptions bias +0.30 at z +5.39, described as the largest
measured effect in the project, deserves the same intercept-versus-direct
check before anyone acts on it.** WR direct alpha here is +0.112.

### THE ONE THING THAT IS NOT FLAT

**Receptions alpha is unstable across seasons.** Direct measurement, p=0.0259:

```
2023  +0.3690     2024  +0.0541     2025  +0.0813     2026  +0.0670
```

2023 is more than four times the others. This corroborates `alpha_by_line_v4`
from a new direction, and it connects to an open puzzle: **every arm of the
candidate rule loses in 2023**, and book composition was the leading
explanation. Alpha being triple in 2023 is consistent with that, since alpha
is measured against a consensus whose book set changed.

Rushing alpha IS stable across seasons (direct p=0.735). So this is a
receptions-specific instability.

**If alpha is ever improved, recency weighting is the lever, not a caliber
table.**

Ten, plus one that spent credits. The pattern is different from September 23:

## WORKING PRINCIPLES

Unchanged: hard reboot before concluding a change did not work; always
`encoding='utf-8'`; never use em dashes; test, do not assume; gate before
sweeping; a t-statistic of +6 is a bug until proven otherwise; plain-English
reasoning before design decisions; one command at a time; import, do not
restate; a script that reimplements an existing procedure must reproduce that
procedure's published output before producing a new number; record the sign
convention in every calibration table; check which parameters a result was
computed under before comparing two results.

Added today:

- **THE MIRRORED FILTER RULE.** The existing rule covers an evaluation
  population inheriting a training filter. The mirror also bites: when the APP
  filters and the INSTRUMENT does not, every calibration table describes a
  population wider than the board. `sigma_refit` scored 1,402 rows the app
  returns `None` for. **Any script that scores the pricing layer must apply
  the app's own projection floor.**
- **VERIFY THAT A CHANGE REGISTERED BEFORE ASKING WHETHER IT HELPED.** An
  identical number where one should have moved is the highest-value signal
  available. It found two separate problems today.
- **A NUMBER THAT IS IDENTICAL TO SIXTEEN DIGITS IS NOT A SMALL EFFECT, IT IS
  THE SAME OBJECT.** Check whether the file was saved before reaching for a
  cache explanation. `inspect.getsource` reads from disk.
- **NEVER MEASURE A SMALL EFFECT THROUGH A ROUNDED OUTPUT.** `project_week`
  rounds to 0.1 and produced a fake "105 of 296 players moved" result.
- **MEASURE ALPHA DIRECTLY AT ZERO DEVIATION, NOT AS A REGRESSION
  INTERCEPT.** The intercept is an extrapolation whose SE depends on where the
  data sits, and it manufactured position effects twice today that vanished on
  direct measurement. It is also the wrong parameter for a zero-slope model.
- **PRINT A MULTIPLICITY THRESHOLD BESIDE EVERY P-VALUE.** Twenty cuts at 0.05
  yields a false positive by chance. Both of today's scripts print Bonferroni
  alongside the raw p.
- **REPORT THE MINIMUM DETECTABLE EFFECT BESIDE EVERY NULL RESULT.** "No
  difference" and "no difference we could see at this n" are different claims
  and only one of them is true.
- **`findstr` SPLITS ON SPACES AND HAS NO `\|`.** A malformed pattern returns
  zero hits, which looks exactly like a real absence.
- **ONE LINE PER `python -c`.** cmd executes anything after a newline as a
  separate command.
- **A TOOL ARTIFACT THAT LOOKS LIKE A FINDING IS THE DANGEROUS KIND.** Three
  of today's ten errors produced plausible-looking output rather than an
  error.

## THE HONEST FRAME

Two days ago the project believed the sigma floor was its worst defect, worth
20-plus calibration points across three markets. That was fiction: the number
1.7 in a dict is a projection cutoff, and rushing and qb_passing use a
distribution family that cannot exhibit the pathology at all.

Yesterday's instrument finding is larger. The evaluation harness has never
measured the app's model, which means three handoffs of model-change
instructions were unexecutable, and Phase 3.4 spent three sessions at the top
of the queue while being worth 0.137 receptions.

What today produced is the real fix, and it was in the place nobody was
looking. The pricing MEAN was wrong, not sigma. Correcting it collapses the
worst calibration band in all four markets, from 18 to 32 points down to 1.5
to 5.3, and it required no new data, no new features, and no change to sigma.
The over-dispersion tilt that two handoffs treated as an open problem was mean
error, exactly as the `dP/dsigma` finding predicted before anyone tested it.

Alongside that, four of five markets stopped claiming edges they cannot
support. That is a visible loss of app functionality and it is the most honest
thing in the change.

The candidate rule is untouched and still the only open route to this being a
betting project. It cannot deploy through the current app yet, but the gap
narrowed today: 4.4 is in, and what remains is the threshold-selection step.

Both parameters were cut by caliber, position, line value, game context and
line source, about twenty tests. Everything came back flat except one thing:
receptions alpha is four times higher in 2023 than in any later season, which
is the same season every arm of the candidate rule loses in. That is now the
most interesting unexplained number in the project.

Ten errors today, four of them wrong predictions that were stated in advance
and refuted within one command. None reached a document with numbers attached,
which is the difference between today and September 23. The apparatus is still
the asset, and it is now measurably better at catching its own operator.
