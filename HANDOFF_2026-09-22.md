## THE HEADLINE, AND IT CHANGED TWICE IN TWO DAYS

**Monday's conclusion:** the model's disagreement with the line carries no
information. Pooled beta -0.079, SE 0.122, CI [-0.319, +0.161], t(beta=1)
= -8.81 on 596 graded bets. Out of sample the projection was WORSE than the
line alone. Yardage line movement was unpredictable (b +0.006, toward share
0.505). The project looked dead as a betting proposition.

**Tuesday's conclusion, on 200x more data:** beta is POSITIVE and strongly
significant. Against consensus closing lines, 8,178 joined rows, 279 game
clusters, 2025 season, walk-forward:

| market | beta | SE | 95% CI | t(b=0) | t(b=1) | alpha | residSD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| receiving | 0.160 | 0.066 | [+0.030, +0.290] | 2.41 | -12.64 | +3.08 | 26.1 |
| receptions | 0.277 | 0.044 | [+0.191, +0.363] | **6.30** | -16.43 | +0.04 | 1.9 |
| rushing | 0.337 | 0.087 | [+0.166, +0.508] | 3.87 | -7.61 | +4.28 | 32.6 |
| qb_passing | 0.402 | 0.198 | [+0.014, +0.790] | 2.03 | -3.02 | -1.55 | 69.1 |
| **pooled (sigma units)** | **0.273** | **0.040** | **[+0.195, +0.351]** | **6.85** | **-18.26** | +0.06 | 1.0 |

Every market positive. Every CI excludes zero. Standard errors clustered by
game, so this is not a clustering artifact (clustering was validated on
synthetic data to widen SEs 1.8x when the regressor is correlated within
cluster).

RMSE out of sample, consensus lines, confirms it: the blend beats the line
alone in all four markets (receiving 26.28 vs 26.42, receptions 1.87 vs 1.89,
rushing 32.83 vs 33.50, qb_passing 69.04 vs 69.29). The RAW projection is
still worse than the line in every market, which is the same finding as
Monday.

**Plain reading:** the model contains real information the market does not
fully price, about 27 percent of its deviation is signal, and the shipped
pricing overstates that by roughly 3.7x.

### Why the two results differ

1. **Sample.** 596 rows (SE 0.122) versus 8,178 (SE 0.040). The CIs barely
   fail to overlap, so this is a sharper instrument rather than a flat
   contradiction.
2. **Selection.** Monday's rows were props Ted chose to log, skewed toward
   large edges. Tuesday used every prop in the season. If beta declines as the
   deviation grows, which the reliability bands suggest, a large-edge sample
   understates it.
3. **DIFFERENT PROJECTIONS, and this is the important caveat.**
   `eval_harness.score_market` refits a plain `LinearRegression` on each
   module's feature list. It is NOT the shipped model. The stored projections
   Monday's test used carried the rushing survivorship bug, the anytime TD
   filter, and weeks 1 to 2 features at their weakest. So Tuesday's result
   says **this feature set beats the line**, not **the deployed app beats the
   line.** Closing that gap is a priority.
4. Two early-season weeks of 2026 versus a full 2025 season.

### Three caveats on Tuesday's result

- **The in-sample comparison printed identical numbers to walk-forward, and is
  therefore meaningless as run.** For season 2025 alone, "train on seasons
  before 2025" and "train through 2024" are the same set. The comparison only
  means anything across multiple seasons. **Run `--all-seasons --in-sample`.**
- **The "best line" beta of 0.345 is partly artifact.** `line_best` is chosen
  USING the model's projection, then appears in both `dev` and `out` with a
  negative sign. Same shared-term structure as the TD movement artifact. A
  placebo test with shuffled projections is needed before citing it as
  shopping value.
- **86 percent join rate** between props and scored player-weeks. The missing
  14 percent is probably players with no stat row, but it has not been
  confirmed non-systematic.

### The alpha finding, possibly more actionable than beta

Receiving alpha **+3.08 yards** and rushing **+4.28** against closing lines.
Receiving outcomes land about 3 yards above the closing line on average
independent of the model.

Three independent estimates now agree in direction:
- +5.40 yards, 2 weeks of 2026, Ted's own captures
- +3.88 yards, 2025 subgroup run (week 1-4 bucket)
- +3.08 yards, full 2025 season against closing lines

At sigma 26 that is about 0.12 sigma, implying an over rate near 54.6 percent
against a -113 breakeven of 53.1 percent. Thin, and right skew pushes the
median below the mean so the real figure is lower. But it requires no model.

Receptions alpha is +0.04, essentially zero, consistent with receptions being
the market where FanDuel prices per-player opinion into the odds rather than
using flat vig.

### Code fixes shipped

1. **`db.py` rewritten.** One authenticated client per session. The bug:
   `get_authed_client` was called once per market, so six `set_session` calls
   per rerun, each burning a single-use refresh token. Later calls got a spent
   token, silently fell back to the anon client, and under RLS scoped
   `TO authenticated` the reads returned **zero rows with no error**. Line
   Movement went blank for every week including ones that had worked. Also
   added: pagination past the 1000-row PostgREST cap, non-silent query
   failures, `compute_p_over`, `diagnose_lines`.

2. **`rushing.actual_result`** no longer reads `build_dataset`, which filters
   `carries >= 5` and `position == "RB"`. Consequences of the old behaviour:
   a back with under 5 carries returned `None` and the bet never graded, and
   **no QB rushing bet could ever grade.** Lost rows averaged 5.0 rushing
   yards against 54.0 for survivors, so missing grades were disproportionately
   busts and the measured record was biased favourably.

3. **`anytime_td.actual_result`** same fix. `touches >= 3` meant any player
   with 0 to 2 touches never graded, and those are almost all non-scorers.
   Three cases now separated explicitly: played and scored -> 100, played and
   did not score -> **0** (a real loss, previously invisible), did not play ->
   `None`.

4. **`anytime_td` split into `build_all_rows` and `build_dataset`.** Rolling
   features now computed over every active game, then filtered. `load_model`
   trains on `touches_roll >= 3` (the SERVING population) read from the
   unfiltered frame. Backtested on 2025: log loss **0.5452 vs 0.5537**, AUC
   **0.6540 vs 0.6388**, calibration gap **+0.3 pp (z +0.39) vs -2.1
   (z -2.81)**. Train rows 9,927, intercept -2.08.

5. **`data_utils._try_seasons`** no longer swallows fetch failures. The old
   `except Exception: continue` treated a future season and a network failure
   identically, and every caller discarded the second return value, so a
   transient failure silently produced a frame roughly a third short. Now
   raises for any season at or below `_current_nfl_season()`, prints a note
   for genuinely unposted future seasons, and raises if nothing loads at all.
   nflreadpy filesystem caching enabled via `update_config(cache_mode=...)`.
   **Baseline: 78,041 player-stat rows across 2022-2026** (18,831 / 18,643 /
   18,983 / 19,422 / 2,162).

## ESTABLISHED FINDINGS (do not re-litigate)

- **Beta is positive.** See the headline table. Pooled 0.273, SE 0.040.
- **The pricing assumption is rejected** at t(beta=1) = -18.26. Edges are
  roughly 3.7x too large.
- **The raw projection is worse than the line** as a mean estimate, in every
  market, out of sample.
- **Selection effect confirmed on the bets table.** Same 407 rows:
  unconditional calibration gap +0.9 pp (z +0.36), but conditioned on the side
  the model picked, **-10.2 pp (z -4.08)**. With 533 rows it reached z -5.01.
  For zero signal the predicted gap is -8.7 pp; observed -10.2. The pricing is
  honest until you condition on choosing the favoured side.
- **Reliability bands are symmetrically overconfident**, not skewed:
  0.20-0.40 band **+20.1 pp (z +4.84)**, 0.40-0.60 band -2.2 (z -0.77),
  0.60-0.80 band **-22.4 pp (z -3.85)**, 0.80-1.00 -43.7 (z -2.00). Overall
  gap +0.9. Tier size is therefore ANTI-predictive as displayed.
- **The under-skew hypothesis is dead.** Week 1 unders -21.5 pp (z -4.12) did
  not replicate: Week 2 unders alone -3.8 pp (z -0.76), and Week 2 overs were
  the worse side. Do NOT refit sigma or the gate on that basis.
- **Yardage line movement is unpredictable by the model.** Pooled b +0.006,
  CI [-0.012, +0.023], toward share 0.505 (p 0.90), flat across all deviation
  buckets. Caveat: the premise test was underpowered (movement to outcome
  b +1.339, SE 1.274), so this is weak evidence rather than a verdict.
- **Anytime TD movement is partly real.** Baseline b +0.0512, placebo mean
  +0.0198 across 200 shuffles, so the honest effect is about **+0.031**. Test
  with the shared term removed gives +0.0253 (t +2.06). These odds series
  mean-revert (b -0.1947, t -2.58), which is the artifact mechanism. The
  direction-only count is null at 0.472, so the effect may be about move SIZE
  rather than direction. A lead, not an edge. **Re-run at Week 5 and Week 8.**
- **Range restriction (Thorndike), not structural failure**, explains the
  within-tier correlation collapse. Observed within-tier correlations are
  roughly what the correction predicts (receiving mean 0.156 against a
  predicted ~0.21).
- **Monte Carlo is premature.** Closed-form gamma and negative binomial are
  exact for marginal single-stat distributions and validated to 1 to 2 pp. MC
  becomes necessary only for JOINT distributions (correlated props, SGP) or a
  play-by-play engine.
- **Receptions is 93 percent flat on the line.** 254 of 273 props showed no
  line movement, because FanDuel moves reception prices through the ODDS.
  Line Movement measures the wrong quantity for that market.
- **Books post anytime TD early and yardage late.** A Tuesday capture returned
  356 rows for the Thursday game and 24 for Monday night; FanDuel had only 6
  QBs and 13 receivers across the slate. Full yardage coverage arrives Friday
  and Saturday. Confirmed not a parse bug: `rows` equals `players` in nearly
  every cell.

## NEXT SESSION: START HERE

### 1. Run `eval_harness.py --all-seasons --in-sample`

**The single most important follow-up.** Tuesday's result is one season. This
tells you whether beta ~0.27 holds across 2023 and 2024, and the in-sample
comparison becomes meaningful across multiple seasons (it was degenerate on a
single season and printed identical numbers).

- If all three seasons show beta near 0.27, the finding is robust and Monday's
  negative was a small selected sample from two early-season weeks.
- If they disagree materially, there is a stability problem and beta should
  not be applied in pricing yet.
- A beta clearly higher in sample than walk-forward means the models are
  fitting noise.

Note this will page all 341,879 rows, so expect it to be slow.

### 2. Compute the edge threshold

Beta gives the shrinkage but not where a shrunk edge clears the vig. Rough
arithmetic: with sigma 26 and a deviation SD around 8, a blended mean moves
about 2 yards, which is roughly 3 percentage points of probability against a
6 percent hold. So the AVERAGE prop is unprofitable and only the tail is
worth betting. **Knowing where that tail starts is the difference between a
usable product and a number on a screen.** This is what turns beta into
Ted's stated vision: loud when there is something to say, silent otherwise.

### 3. Make the harness score the ACTUAL models

`score_market` currently refits a plain `LinearRegression` on each module's
feature list. Switch it to call each module's own `load_model` where possible.
Two reasons: Tuesday's result would then describe the deployed app rather than
the feature set, and Phase 3 fixes could be measured directly instead of by
proxy.

### 4. Placebo-test the "best line" beta

0.345 against 0.273 for consensus. `line_best` is selected using the model's
projection and then appears in both `dev` and `out`, which is the same
shared-term structure that inflated the TD movement result. Shuffle the
projections and refit; the placebo mean is the artifact, and the honest
shopping value is the difference.

### 5. Then Phase 3 fixes, each measured against the harness

Ordered by expected impact:

- **3.1 rushing `carries_roll` survivorship.** `build_dataset` filters
  `carries >= 5` BEFORE the rolling mean, so a backup's workload feature comes
  only from weeks the starter was hurt. Inflation is **+6.31 carries in the
  0-2 bucket, falling monotonically to +0.06 at 15+**. The training population
  is also wrong: surviving rows average **54.0** rushing yards against a true
  unconditional **32.5**, so the intercept is inflated by roughly 66 percent
  for backups. This produced Justice Hill at 39.9 against a 13.5 line (he ran
  for 6), Samaje Perine 45.0 against 16.5, Chris Rodriguez 52.8 against 24.5.
  Apply the same shape as the anytime TD fix: roll over all active games, then
  filter, and train on the serving population.
- **3.2 refit the rushing sigma and floor.** `1.922 * proj^0.7149` and floor
  34.5 were fitted against the OLD projections. Non-optional consequence of
  3.1.
- **3.3 qb_passing `attempts >= 10`.** Same survivorship shape, removes the
  left tail (benched, injured, blowout), and blocks grading. Likely mechanism
  behind the +8.81 bias. The bias is slope-shaped (+1.06 to +10.48 across
  quintiles) so a level fix is wrong.
- **3.4 `receiving` and `receptions` `actual_result`** return `None` where 0
  is correct for an active player. Neither applies a volume filter, so this is
  smaller than the rushing and TD versions.
- **3.5 WR receptions bias +0.30 (z +5.39).** Best-supported single
  correction available.
- **3.6 RB receiving as a ypt problem**, not volume.
- **3.7 the `qb_rushing` market label.** `MARKET_MAP` in `import_lines` has no
  `qb_rushing` key, so QB rushing props store as `market = 'rushing'`. The
  `is_qb_model` flag routes the edge calculation correctly in memory but never
  reaches the database. **The API data has the same issue**, since
  `player_rush_yds` contains both RB and QB rushing. Solve it once at analysis
  time by splitting on position from nflverse after the join, rather than at
  fetch time.
- **3.8 audit every `build_dataset` filter for survivorship.** Three instances
  found by looking at six files.
- **3.9 the 7-row receiving anomaly**: predicted 0.218, actual 0.857, z +4.83.

### 6. Then Phase 5.1, pricing off the blended mean

`line + beta * (projection - line)` as the distribution mean, beta
configurable, **defaulting to the measured value rather than 0**. Monday's
plan said beta 0 would collapse every edge to the vig baseline; Tuesday's
result means it is ~0.27 and the product survives. Use market-specific betas,
which three seasons now supports.

Also: **residual SD from the harness is the correct sigma** for pricing around
a blended mean (26.1 receiving, 1.9 receptions, 32.6 rushing, 69.1
qb_passing). The shipped sigma was fitted as spread around the model's own
projection and therefore includes the projection error. And apply **alpha per
market** (+3.08 receiving, +4.28 rushing, +0.04 receptions, -1.55
qb_passing), the mean-versus-median offset estimated from real lines rather
than guessed.

### 7. Line-as-feature model, now more attractive

```
actual = alpha + gamma*line + sum(delta_i * feature_i)
```

Beta 0.273 is a single scalar saying "27 percent of the deviation is real,
always." That is almost certainly wrong in an interesting way: some features
carry information the market misses, others are already fully priced. Delta
tells them apart, so instead of shrinking everything uniformly you learn WHICH
features to lean on. Today's positive beta is the evidence there is something
to find. **Fit this AFTER the Phase 3 fixes**, so the features being tested
are the honest versions.

### Gotchas encountered today

- **`getpass` shows nothing as you type.** Right-click pastes in cmd. A new
  paste appends rather than overwriting, so Ctrl+C and restart is cleaner than
  trying to clear.
- **Historical endpoints are paid-plan only**, including the historical EVENTS
  endpoint. A free dry run of the backfill is impossible; the 401s cost
  nothing but the script grinds through all 24 before giving up. Worth making
  it stop on the first 401.
- **The 2025 enumeration returned all 272 regular season games from a single
  September call.** Consequence: `opening_date` is September 1 for nearly
  every game, so an `--snapshots opening` run would query odds from September
  for a December game. **The opening snapshot needs a per-game offset (e.g.
  kickoff minus 3 days) rather than "when the event first appeared."** Not yet
  fixed. Closing lines are the more important half anyway.
- `fill_weeks.py` is the slowest script in the suite: 833 sequential updates,
  each rewriting every row for that event. A single SQL update from a temp
  table of event-to-week pairs would run in seconds.
- 5 of 284 games in 2025 returned no props at the closing timestamp. Real
  property of the data, not a failure. 98 percent coverage.

### Working principles (Ted's, earned)

- **Hard reboot before concluding a change did not work.** Streamlit caches
  `build_dataset` and `load_model`.
- **Always `encoding='utf-8'`** in parse checks, and always parse-check before
  pushing.
- **Never use em dashes** in any writing. Firm.
- **Test, do not assume.** Silent failures (the snap merge, null projections,
  the inverted side, the refresh token, the survivorship filters) have
  consistently been more dangerous than noisy ones.
- **Gate before sweeping.** Measure whether an effect exists before tuning its
  parameter. This killed four ideas that looked good on point estimates, and
  today it killed two of Claude's hypotheses: the TD survivorship bug was real
  but was NOT causing the calibration failure, and unconditional training
  overcorrected.
- Ted wants plain-English reasoning before design decisions, pushes back when
  framing feels off, and is frequently right. He values being corrected over
  validated. He caught the "most bet market" mischaracterisation of anytime TD
  (it is the most GRADED market, because it has the most players, not the most
  wagered) and he correctly objected to limiting live capture to 96 hours.
- Writes handoff docs at context limits.
- On mobile, prefers whole-file replacements over surgical edits.

## THE HONEST FRAME

Ted built a working NFL projection system from no Python background, then in
two days established with real statistical rigour that it contains a small but
genuine edge the market does not fully price, and that his own app was
overstating that edge by nearly four times. He did it by building the
measurement infrastructure first and letting it kill his own hypotheses,
including a full day where the answer looked like zero.

The project is no longer open-loop. There are 341,879 rows of multi-book
closing lines, a harness that answers the central question in minutes, and a
measured beta with a standard error of 0.040.

What remains is engineering: fix the known bugs, measure each one, apply the
shrinkage, and find where the shrunk edge clears the vig. That last number is
the product.
